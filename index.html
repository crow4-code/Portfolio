My love for game design started young, right after I got my first console — an Xbox 360. I'd rush to play it every day after school, but what really stuck with me wasn't just playing games, it was imagining how they were made. Over time, that curiosity turned into something more: the real thrill for me is building something, testing it, and feeling proud of what I created.

I'm especially drawn to horror and psychological games — experiences that get under your skin and make you think — as well as open-world, story-driven games where players have the freedom to explore and shape their own path.

Right now, I'm working on an idea I've had for a while — a horror game inspired by Little Nightmares, but darker and more visceral. It centers on psychological torment, blending unsettling side effects with an oppressive, dread-filled atmosphere. I'm exploring a soundtrack built around the names of side effects themselves, using them almost like a haunting motif throughout the game. There's also a relentless creature (or human) stalking the player — designed to feel unpredictable, with jump scares that keep you constantly on edge rather than following a pattern you can anticipate.
